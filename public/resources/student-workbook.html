Quantum Explorer

Student Investigation Workbook · Year 10 · Four approximately 60-minute lessons

Make a prediction before running the experiment. Record what actually happened, then explain it. No account or identifying information is required. Open the interactive app at tolverne.github.io/quantum-explorer/.

Lesson 1 · What is a qubit?

Big question: How can we prepare a qubit in more than just two states?

1 · A quantum bit flip

Prediction: What happens to |0⟩ under X(+180°)?

Ends at |1⟩ Stays at |0⟩ Points +X

Experiment: Free Explorer → Reset → X(+180°), then replay. Change your camera view.

Observation:

Explanation: How is rotating the qubit different from moving the camera?

2 · Beyond zero and one

Prediction: Where does Y(+90°) take |0⟩?

South +X equator Stays +Z

Experiment: Reset, Y(+90°), then try Z rotation on the equator.

Observation:

Explanation: What new possibility does the Bloch sphere show?

3 · Rotating around itself

Prediction: What will Z(+90°) do to |0⟩?

Move +X Move |1⟩ No visible change

Experiment: Rotate around Z at the north pole, then after preparing |+⟩.

Observation:

Explanation: Why do the two cases differ?

4 · Two-pulse route

Prediction: Does Y then Z always equal Z then Y?

Always Not generally Only negative angles

Experiment: Target Challenges 5 and 6; record an ordered pulse sequence.

Observation:

Explanation: Why does order matter?

5 · Four extra challenges

Prediction: Can the same Y(+90°) rotation lead to different end states depending on the start?

Yes No Only camera angle matters

Experiment: Try two of Bloch Challenge Missions 7–10. Record an ordered pulse sequence for each.

Observation (mission numbers and pulses):

Explanation: What changed with the initial state, rotation direction, or restricted axes?

Lesson 2 · What is measurement?

Big question: How can a known quantum state give an unpredictable outcome?

1 · A predictable measurement

Prediction: What does |0⟩ give when measured in Z?

Always 0 Always 1 50/50

Experiment: Measurement Lab → prepare |0⟩, Z, measure once and run 100 fresh shots. Repeat for |1⟩.

Observation:

Explanation: Why is this case predictable?

2 · Known state, uncertain result

Prediction: What happens to |+⟩ in Z?

Always 0 Always 1 Either, 50/50

Experiment: Prepare |+⟩, measure once and then compare 10, 100, 1,000 fresh shots.

Observation:

Explanation: How does probability differ from a particular outcome?

3 · The same qubit, twice

Prediction: If Z measurement gave 1, what will immediate repeated Z measurement give?

0 1 50/50 again

Experiment: Measure a prepared |+⟩ twice without re-preparing; compare with the Prepare again button.

Observation:

Explanation: Why do repeated and fresh measurements differ?

4 · Change measuring axis

Prediction: What does |+⟩ give in X?

Always + Always − 50/50

Experiment: Compare |+⟩ in Z and X; then |0⟩ in both bases.

Observation:

Explanation: Why must we specify the measurement basis?

Lesson 3 · What is a mixed state?

Big question: What does a shorter Bloch vector mean?

1 · Pure states on the surface

Prediction: If every run prepares |0⟩, where is the ensemble Bloch vector?

North pole Centre Halfway south

Experiment: Mixed State Lab → All |0⟩. Compare measurements in Z and X. Try All |+⟩.

Observation:

Why is a random Z result from pure |+⟩ NOT the same as being at the centre?

2 · An uncertain preparation

Prediction: A source prepares 80% |0⟩ and 20% |1⟩. What happens in Z?

Always 0 80/20 distribution 50/50

Experiment: Set the mixture to 80% |0⟩; notice its length is 0.6. Run fresh shots in X and Z.

Observation:

Why is the vector shorter? Is anything still predictable?

3 · At the centre

Prediction: An equal |0⟩/|1⟩ mixture gives what in X and Z?

Always 0 in Z 50/50 only in Z 50/50 in both

Experiment: Set weight to 50%. Check centre dot and 100/1,000 fresh trials in both bases.

Observation:

Why is the centre different from a surface point?

4 · Different ways to make the centre

Prediction: Is an equal |+⟩/|−⟩ mixture equivalent to equal |0⟩/|1⟩?

No Yes Only in Z

Experiment: Compare 50/50 X and Z mixtures, then try 80/20 X mixtures.

Observation:

How might this help explain the individual qubits of an entangled pair?

Lesson 4 · What is entanglement?

Big question: What do joint measurements reveal that local qubits cannot?

1 · Independent qubits

Prediction: Two separate |+⟩ qubits in Z/Z?

Only 00/11 All four equally Only 01/10

Experiment: Entanglement Lab → Independent → Z/Z 100 pairs, then X/X.

Observation:

Explanation: Why isn't always seeing ++ in X/X proof of entanglement?

2 · Two random classical bits

Prediction: Alice and Bob each choose a random bit independently. How often do they match?

Always Half the time Never

Experiment: Select Two random bits. Run 100 pairs in Z/Z, then X/X. Which four joint outcomes appear?

Observation:

How is this different from COPYING one random bit to both people?

3 · Matched classical bits

Prediction: A fair random bit is copied to Alice and Bob. What happens in Z/Z?

Always match Always differ Four equal

Experiment: Select Classically matched bits → compare Z/Z and X/X with Two random bits.

Observation:

Explanation: Why are matching Z results not enough?

4 · The Bell-pair mystery

Prediction: Bell Φ+ in X/X?

Only ++/−− All four equal Only +−/−+

Experiment: Bell Φ+ → Z/Z and X/X, then measure Alice before Bob on one pair.

Observation:

Explanation: How can local qubits be random but joint results agree?

5 · An opposite correlation

Prediction: Bell Φ− in X/X?

Match Disagree Four equal

Experiment: Bell Φ− → compare Z/Z and X/X; compare to Φ+.

Observation:

Explanation: How do two bases distinguish entanglement from the simple classical model?
